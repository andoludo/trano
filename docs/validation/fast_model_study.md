# FastModel against Buildings: BESTEST and a renovated house

This page compares the `FastModel` library with Buildings on the three signals a heating study or a calibration
rests on: the **zone air temperatures**, the **heating power** hour by hour and the **heating demand** over the
period, and asks a second question that the [replay validation](fast_model.md) does not: when a parameter of the
YAML changes, does FastModel move the way Buildings moves? A reduced model is fit for design studies and for
calibration only if the derivative of its results with respect to the parameters is right, not only its results at
one point. The [improvement plan](fast_model_improvement_plan.md) draws the consequences.

## What is compared and how

Both libraries are generated by trano from the **same YAML file** and simulated by **OpenModelica in the same
container** (FastModel is a Modelica library; its CasADi export plays no part here). The FastModel model runs in
closed loop with its own controllers, as the Buildings model runs with its own: nothing is replayed, the two models
are two simulations of the same building description. The hourly means of the zone air temperatures, of the heat
delivered to each zone by its emitters (ideal system or radiators), of the electricity of the heat pump and the fans
and of the PV production are extracted from both result files by `validation/fast_model` and committed with their
KPIs; the tables and figures below are rendered from those files.

The cases, chosen among the complex ones:

| Case | What it is | Why it is here |
|---|---|---|
| **BESTEST 950FF** | the heavy zone of ASHRAE 140 (concrete block walls, concrete slab), free floating, with a night ventilation fan of 1700 m3/h from 18:00 to 07:00 | the free-floating case with the most physics: thermal mass, solar gains through 12 m2 of south glazing, a scheduled air flow that no controller corrects |
| **BESTEST 940** | the heavy zone with ideal heating to 20 degC by day and 10 degC at night (ramp from 07:00 to 08:00) and cooling at 27 degC | the most complete BESTEST system with heating: a set-back schedule makes the morning reheat peak depend on the mass and the control |
| **BESTEST 960** | the light zone heated and cooled behind an unconditioned heavy sun-space, coupled by a common wall | two zones, an internal wall, a passive zone driven by the sun |
| **Residential, after renovation** | [`examples/residential_after_renovation.yaml`](residential_reference_cases.md): six zones, air-to-water heat pump with a 100 l buffer, radiators with thermostatic valves, balanced ventilation with heat recovery, PV, Uccle | every system FastModel models, in closed loop, over 28 days of January |

For each case the **perturbations** change one field of the YAML and are simulated with both libraries; the table
"Response to the perturbations" gives, for each KPI, the change seen by Buildings, the change seen by FastModel and
their ratio (1.0 would be a perfect agreement on the sensitivity, whatever the offset between the two libraries):

| Case | Envelope perturbations | System perturbations |
|---|---|---|
| 950FF | insulation conductivity x1.5, infiltration x2, wall mass x0.5 | |
| 940 | the same | heating set points +2 K |
| residential_after | EPS conductivity x1.5, n50 x2 | heat pump 2 kW instead of 3 kW, supply set point +10 K, radiators x0.7, buffer tank x3, heat recovery 0.5 instead of 0.8, living room and kitchen set point +1 K |

The KPIs: mean, lowest and highest hourly zone temperature; heating and cooling demand (the heat delivered to the
zones); peak heating (largest hourly mean); the heat pump electricity and the OpenModelica time. Hour by hour, the
FastModel temperatures are compared with the Buildings ones by their RMSE, bias and largest error, and the heating
power by the CV(RMSE) and NMBE of ASHRAE Guideline 14.

The night ventilation of 950FF is modelled by `ventilationSchedule` in trano's Buildings zone; the FastModel zone
did not take that parameter until this study (it silently dropped the fan), it does now.
