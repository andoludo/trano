    A house before and after renovation

    This tutorial models a whole house twice: a Belgian semi-detached house built before 1970, and the same house after a deep renovation. It brings together what the previous tutorials introduced (several zones, a hydronic heating system, ventilation, PV) and shows the outputs trano puts on the data bus: zone temperatures, heat, gas and electricity.

    The two configuration files are in the examples folder of the repository:

    • residential_before_renovation.yaml: uninsulated cavity walls and attic floor, old double glazing, a leaky envelope (n50 = 12 1/h), a 16 kW gas boiler on radiators, natural ventilation.
    • residential_after_renovation.yaml: external wall insulation, 22 cm on the attic floor, an insulated slab, triple glazing, n50 = 3 1/h, a 3 kW air-water heat pump on the same radiators, balanced ventilation with heat recovery and 4.2 kWp of PV.

    Both are written by examples/residential_cases.py from one description of the house, so that they share everything but the envelope, the airtightness and the systems. The sizing and the comparison of the two libraries are detailed in the residential reference cases.

    The house

    Six zones on two storeys (110 m2): the living room with the hall and the kitchen on the ground floor, three bedrooms and the bathroom upstairs. The street facade faces north, the garden south, the gable west; the east wall is shared with the neighbour.

    Each zone is a space of the infiltration variant that takes its airtightness, n50, from which both libraries derive the same infiltration (n50 / 20). The living room of the renovated house:

      - id: LIVING:001
    variant: infiltration
    occupancy:
      parameters:
        occupancy: 3600*{7, 8, 17, 23}
    parameters:
      floor_area: 40.0
      average_room_height: 2.6
      n50: 3.0
    external_boundaries:
      external_walls:
        - {surface: 19.24, azimuth: 0.0, tilt: wall, construction: EXTERNAL_WALL:001}
        - {surface: 14.04, azimuth: 1.5708, tilt: wall, construction: EXTERNAL_WALL:001}
        - {surface: 7.8, azimuth: 3.1416, tilt: wall, construction: EXTERNAL_WALL:001}
      windows:
        - {surface: 6.5, azimuth: 0.0, tilt: wall, construction: WINDOW:001, frame_fraction: 0.25}
        - {surface: 1.5, azimuth: 1.5708, tilt: wall, construction: WINDOW:001, frame_fraction: 0.25}
        - {surface: 0.6, azimuth: 3.1416, tilt: wall, construction: WINDOW:001, frame_fraction: 0.25}
      adiabatic_walls:
        - {surface: 21.84, azimuth: -1.5708, tilt: wall, construction: PARTY_WALL:001}
      floor_on_grounds:
        - surface: 40.0
          construction: GROUND_FLOOR:001
          perimeter: 15.8
          average_indoor_temperature: 293.15
          ground_temperature: 286.65

    Three parts of this description are worth a closer look.

    • The party wall is an adiabatic_walls entry: a wall whose outer face exchanges no heat because the neighbour is heated alike. Its construction is half of the shared brick wall; it stores heat in the zone but loses none. IDEAS renders it as an adiabatic BoundaryWall, Buildings as a construction of the zone whose outer face is left open.
    • The windows are described by their panes and gaps (glazings). trano computes their U and g values for IDEAS (EN 673, EN 410) from the same layers that Buildings uses.
    • The slab on ground is where the two libraries differ most. IDEAS models the ground below the slab (ISO 13370) from the exposed perimeter and the mean temperature of the room above, average_indoor_temperature; Buildings holds the outer face of the slab at ground_temperature. examples/residential_cases.py chooses that temperature so that both give the same heat flow over the heating season. These are the only library-specific inputs of the files.

    The systems

    Before renovation a gas boiler heats the radiators; after renovation an air-water heat pump does, with a balanced ventilation unit that recovers heat from the extract air, and PV on the roof:

    systems:
  - boiler:
      id: GENERATOR:001
      control:
        boiler_control:
          id: GENERATOR_CONTROL:001
          variant: simple
      # air-water heat pump rated 3 kW at -8 degC and 40 degC supply (heating curve down to 30 degC at
      # 16 degC outdoor), 100 l buffer
      parameters:
        nominal_heating_power: 3000
        temperature_supply_setpoint_heat_pump: 313.15
        use_storage_tank: true
        tank_volume: 0.1
        height_of_tank_without_insulation: 0.8
      variant: air_water_heat_pump
  - air_handling_unit:
      id: AHU:001
      variant: system_d
      parameters:
        m_flow_nominal: 0.05
        heat_exchanger_effectiveness: 0.8
solar:
  - photovoltaic:
      id: PV:001
      parameters:
        area: 21.0
        efficiency: 0.2
        azimuth: 0.0
        tilt: 35.0

    The heat pump runs when a room asks for heat (the simple boiler control watches the radiator valves) and the outdoor air is below 16 degC. It charges its buffer to a supply temperature that follows a heating curve, from 40 degC at -8 degC outdoor down to 30 degC at 16 degC, and draws its heat from the outdoor air of the weather file.

    Generating and simulating the models

    One file gives the model of either library:

    from trano.main import create_model, simulate_model

create_model("examples/residential_after_renovation.yaml", library="IDEAS")
create_model("examples/residential_after_renovation.yaml", library="Buildings")

simulate_model(
    "examples/residential_after_renovation.yaml",
    library="Buildings",
    start=0,
    end=7 * 24 * 3600,
)

    Outputs on the data bus

    Besides the zone temperatures, the systems publish their results on the data bus of the model, where controllers, reports and other tools find them:

    Element Outputs
    Gas boiler fuel_power (on the gas bus), fuel_energy [kWh], heat_flow, heat_energy [kWh], gas_usage [m3], supply and return temperatures
    Air-water heat pump power (on the grid load), electric_energy [kWh], heat_flow, heat_energy [kWh], supply, return and buffer temperatures, compressor_speed
    Ventilation unit power of the fans (on the grid load), electric_energy [kWh], recovered_heat_flow, supply_temperature
    PV pv_power, pv_energy [kWh]
    Pumps power (on the grid load)

    Results

    A January week

    The first week of January (Buildings). Before renovation the boiler burns up to 9 kW of gas to bring the house back to temperature every morning; the living room and the kitchen are held at 21 degC, the bedrooms at 18 degC and the bathroom reaches 22 degC morning and evening.

    After renovation the heat pump delivers about 2 kW at most and draws less than 1 kW of electricity. The house cools slowly at night: the bedrooms barely leave 18-20 degC. The bathroom, heated by its old radiator with water at 30-40 degC, struggles to reach its set point in the morning: a known drawback of keeping high-temperature radiators with a heat pump and a setback. The PV produces on sunny afternoons, out of step with the morning peak of the heat pump.

    A year

    Over the year, the renovation divides the heat delivered to the rooms by seven, from 130 to 18 kWh/m2, and replaces 19.2 MWh of gas by 0.53 MWh of electricity for the heat pump (seasonal COP 3.8) and 0.22 MWh for the fans. IDEAS and Buildings agree on these within 7.5 %:
