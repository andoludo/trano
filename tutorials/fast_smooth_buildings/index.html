    FastSmoothBuildings: models close to Buildings, in closed loop and for MPC

    The FastSmoothBuildings library turns a YAML file into a model close to the Buildings library that still translates into a CasADi ODE (with rumoca) for simulation, state estimation and model predictive control with IPOPT:

    • every zone has the physics of Buildings.ThermalZones.Detailed.MixedAir: layered walls, floors and roofs, windows with the angular optics of their glazing, interior and exterior convection, long-wave exchange with the sky and between the room surfaces, the transmitted solar radiation distributed on the room surfaces, infiltration, walls and floors shared between zones;
    • the hydronic circuits of the YAML are modelled: radiators (EN 442), valves, mixing three-way valves, split valves, pumps, sensors, gas boilers and air-to-water heat pumps with their buffer;
    • balanced ventilation units with heat recovery (trano's system_d) and PV arrays.

    The library has two configurations, built from the same components and templates:

    • FastSmoothBuildings simulates the building in closed loop, as the Buildings and IDEAS models do: the controllers of the YAML are part of the model (written without events) and the water flows follow the pressures of the circuits;
    • FastSmoothBuildingsMPC is the model for optimal control: the controllers are left out, the controls of the systems are inputs that the optimisation decides, and each valve sets the flow of its branch.
    trano create-model house.yaml FastSmoothBuildings       # closed loop: writes house.mo
trano simulate-model house.yaml FastSmoothBuildings     # runnable, like the other libraries
trano create-model house.yaml FastSmoothBuildingsMPC    # the controls as inputs, for optimal control

    A library like Buildings and IDEAS

    FastSmoothBuildings is used exactly as trano uses Buildings and IDEAS: a Modelica library and one template per element of the YAML, without code of its own.

    • trano/library/FastSmoothBuildings.mo holds the components (the physics), embedded in every generated package;
    • trano/elements/library/models/fast_smooth_buildings/*.yaml hold the templates that declare a component for each element of the YAML, with its ports, as the templates of the other libraries;
    • the FastSmoothBuildings entry of library.json writes the constructions and glazings as records of a Data package, takes the parameter names of Buildings, and the package template templates/fast_smooth_buildings.jinja2; the FastSmoothBuildingsMPC entry extends it under another name, and the templates render each element for the library they are given (library == 'FastSmoothBuildings': controllers and pressure-driven circuits; 'FastSmoothBuildingsMPC': inputs).

    The walls, windows and floors are elements of their own connected to the bus of their zone, as in IDEAS: each OuterWall, Window, BoundaryWall (floor on ground or over outdoor air), AdiabaticWall or InternalWall connects its propsBus_a to a propsBus[i] of its Zone. The bus carries the convective and radiative exchanges with the room, the properties of the surface that the zone needs (area, emissivity, solar absorptance and transmittance), the solar radiation a window transmits, and the weather.

    Package Components
    BoundaryConditions Weather (solar position and Perez sky of the site), TiltedSurface (irradiance on a surface)
    Envelope OuterWall, Window, BoundaryWall, AdiabaticWall, InternalWall
    Zones Zone, VentilatedZone
    Hydronics Radiator (EN 442), Valve, ThreeWayValve, Junction, Pump, TemperatureSensor, Boiler, HeatPump
    Ventilation HeatRecoveryUnit, Duct
    Electrical Photovoltaic
    HVAC IdealHeatingCooling (heat as an input), IdealHeater, IdealHeatingCoolingPI (closed loop)
    Controls EmissionP, EmissionPI, EmissionOnOff, EmissionMonitored, CollectorControl, ThreeWayValveControl, BoilerDemand, BoilerTemperatureControl, HeatPumpControl (closed loop, without events)

    The generated package holds two models:

    • building_mpc, the building: an explicit ODE whose inputs are the weather data, the internal gains and, with FastSmoothBuildingsMPC, the controls of the systems. It contains no table, no event and no component of another library;
    • building, the runnable model: building_mpc fed with the weather file (the Buildings reader, which also gives building_mpc the location of the site), the occupancy schedules, and the external data of the network: the controls replayed from it with FastSmoothBuildingsMPC (each held at 1 without data), the set points of the MonitoredSetpoint emission controls with FastSmoothBuildings.

    Every component carries the icon of the Buildings model it follows (valves, pumps, radiators, boilers, heat pumps, walls, windows, zones, weather...), and its ports are placed on that icon, so that the model reads in a Modelica editor as a Buildings model does.

    building_mpc is built as the Buildings and IDEAS models are, by the same code and configuration: its components are grouped in the containers of containers.yaml (envelope, emission, distribution, production, bus, ventilation, solar), which building_mpc extends as Components.Containers.building. The containers are linked by array ports (heatPortCon[n], port_a[n]...) of the FastSmoothBuildings connector types that library.json gives (connectors), and all share one data bus, dataBus. As with the other libraries, the components exchange their signals on the data bus: the data server of the bus container writes the air temperature of each zone, an emission control reads it and writes the opening of its valve, the collector control of the circuit reads the openings... and the systems outside the envelope read the weather there. A signal that no component writes is an input of building_mpc when its component says so (model_input in its YAML template: the weather data, the internal gains, the controls of FastSmoothBuildingsMPC); the inputs are at the top of building_mpc, connected to the data bus. The data bus is the expandable connector of the other libraries with the signals of the network declared (data_bus_template), as rumoca does not add members to an expandable connector. The states carry the container in their names (envelope1.space_001.TAir). The containers do not change the equations; leave them out with network.model(include_container=False) or trano create-model house.yaml FastSmoothBuildings --no-containers for a single flat building_mpc.

    Rules for an explicit ODE

    Every component keeps a few rules, so that rumoca translates the assembled model into xdot = f(t, x, u, p):

    • every node joined by connections has a heat capacity, or is solved in closed form inside its component (the radiant star of a room, the mixing of water flows);
    • the equations are smooth: power laws and table interpolations are regularised, conditions are inside functions; the controllers have no event (a hysteresis is a fast bistable state, a PI controller has anti-windup);
    • with FastSmoothBuildingsMPC, flows are set: each valve sets the flow of its branch, a mixing valve the share of supply water, mass balances the rest; the production (boiler, heat pump) or the ventilation unit closes the balance of its circuit, one per circuit. With FastSmoothBuildings, they are pressure-driven: each component has a pressure node of small compliance at its inlet, every flow follows from the pressures, the pumps run on their curves;
    • a port with several connections is an array, one element per connection, sized by the template from the connections trano made: the inlets of a component mix, its outlets leave at its outlet temperature;
    • parameters computed by functions (the conductance of a radiator solved as Buildings does at initialisation, the polynomial fits of the window optics, the scaling of the heat pump tables) take values settled at translation.

    Inputs of building_mpc

    The closed-loop FastSmoothBuildings takes the weather, the internal gains and, for a MonitoredSetpoint emission control, its set point <control>_TSet; FastSmoothBuildingsMPC takes the controls too:

    Input Role
    weather_inputs.TDryBul, .TBlaSky, .winSpe, .winDir, .HDirNor, .HDifHor, .HGloHor weather data, as in a weather file or a forecast (disturbances)
    <occupancy>_gai.QCon, .QRad convective and radiant internal gains per floor area [W/m2] (disturbances)
    <valve>_y.y flow of a radiator branch relative to its nominal flow (control, 0 to 1)
    <three-way valve>_y.y share of supply water (control)
    <boiler>_y.y boiler part load ratio, or heat pump compressor speed (control)
    <ventilation unit>_y.y air flow relative to the design flow (control held at 1 by its bounds, as Buildings runs it)
    <emitter>_Q.Q_flow heat of an ideal emitter [W] (control, between its bounds)

    The solar position and the irradiance on each surface are computed inside the model from the time (the clock time of the year) and the site, so that a forecast only needs the weather channels. The meters of the systems are states in kWh: EFue (boiler fuel), EEle (heat pump electricity), EFan (fans), EPro (PV production).

    Simulation, estimation and optimal control: trano_optimization

    The CasADi side lives in trano-optimization (trano_optimization), a package of its own that depends on rumoca, CasADi, NumPy and pydantic only, not on trano; install it next to trano (pip install trano-optimization). It reads everything from the Modelica model: the names of the states, inputs and parameters in the order of the CasADi vectors, their units, bounds and descriptions. The roles of the variables follow simple conventions (Roles.infer): an input with a min and a max is a control, any other input a disturbance; the states named TAir are the comfort temperatures; the states in kWh are meters.

    from pathlib import Path

import numpy as np

from trano.data_models.conversion import convert_network
from trano.elements.library.library import Library
from trano_optimization import KELVIN, CasadiModel, OptimalControl, OptimalControlSettings

network = convert_network("house", Path("house.yaml"), library=Library.from_configuration("FastSmoothBuildingsMPC"))
model = CasadiModel.from_modelica(network.model(), "house.building_mpc")
ocp = OptimalControl(model, settings=OptimalControlSettings(time_step=3600))

hours = np.arange(24)
comfort = np.where((hours >= 7) & (hours < 22), KELVIN + 20, KELVIN + 16)
disturbances = ...  # (len(ocp.roles.disturbances), 24): forecasts of the weather and gains
result = ocp.solve(model.description.initial_state, disturbances, comfort, price=np.full(24, 0.10),
                   start_time=t0)  # the model time of the first step: the solar position depends on it
result.controls  # branch flows, valve positions, boiler part load ratio, hour by hour

    OptimalControl collocates the ODE on Radau points (stable for the stiff water nodes whatever the step), solves with IPOPT (SPRAL when CasADi ships it, MUMPS otherwise), warm-starts from a simulation of the model (CVODES), and costs the energy read from the meters: the PV production first covers the electricity of the step, the rest is sold at feed_in_price; the boiler fuel has its own fuel_price. The same discretization gives the moving horizon estimation and the closed loop:

    from trano_optimization import MovingHorizonEstimation, receding_horizon

estimator = MovingHorizonEstimation(model)
estimate = estimator.estimate(prior_state, applied_controls, past_disturbances, measured_air_temperatures)
loop = receding_horizon(ocp, estimate.current, disturbances, comfort, price, horizon=12, start_time=t0)

    The parameters computed from others (a conductance from a thickness and a conductivity) are parameters of their own in the CasADi function, at their values: an identification adjusts the effective ones (conductances, capacities).

    How close to Buildings

    The study of FastSmoothBuildings against Buildings lives with the library, in the FastSmoothBuildings repository (study/): it simulates the Buildings model of a YAML file and compares it with FastSmoothBuildings, replaying what the systems of Buildings did through the runnable FastSmoothBuildingsMPC model or running the closed-loop FastSmoothBuildings with its own controllers, and it measures how both libraries respond to changes of the YAML. See its validation pages for the results.

    Limits

    • One production (boiler or heat pump) per hydraulic circuit, and mixing three-way valves (the bypass from the return): diverting valves are not modelled yet.
    • Ventilation units are balanced (supply = extract) and of the system_d kind.
    • Boilers with a storage tank, water-to-water heat pumps, chillers, domestic hot water, batteries, EV chargers and VAV systems are not modelled: generating a model with them stops with a message naming them.
    • With FastSmoothBuildingsMPC, flows are set by the valves; the pressure-driven sharing of flow between branches is modelled in the closed loop only, with the resistances linearised at their nominal flows.
    • The closed-loop controllers have no event: a hysteresis switches within 10 s, and the minimum on and off times of the heat pump are not modelled.
